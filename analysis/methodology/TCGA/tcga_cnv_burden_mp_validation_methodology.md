# Single-cell CNV burden and TCGA MP comparison

## Purpose and interpretation

`analysis/TCGA/Auto_tcga_cnv_burden_mp_validation.R` asks whether malignant cells with relatively greater inferred CNV signal within a tumour have different MP activity, and whether greater DNA copy-number burden across TCGA EAC tumours has a concordant MP association. These are different estimands: a within-tumour cell contrast and a between-tumour sample contrast. Shared direction supports an association across contexts but is not a direct replication of one statistical parameter. No common numerical burden threshold or absolute MP scale is assumed across assays.

The current primary presentation is a three-page PDF combining continuous correlations with pooled scATLAS and TCGA clinical-style boxplots. A retained side-by-side forest plot of standardized high-minus-low MP effects and sample-bootstrap 95% intervals provides a separate comparison of direction and uncertainty while preserving the different sampling units. Clinical-style supporting boxplots show all 17 MPs along the x-axis with low/high groups dodged side by side. Each scATLAS point is a raw within-sample/group mean UCell score; TCGA shows one point per tumour in its original GSVA units. The two clinical-style pages are now embedded after the continuous heatmap rather than emitted as separate paired/per-study PDFs. Standardization is used for the cross-cohort forest effects, not the supporting boxplot y axes, preserving sample-level baseline heterogeneity. The continuous-rho page retains individual-study columns, with pooled scATLAS and TCGA separated into their own bordered facets and explicit gutters. Interpretation is not tied only to dichotomization.

## Burden definitions and grouping

For scATLAS, split independently within each `orig.ident` at the median per-cell native InferCNA signal. Low cells have signal at or below the median; high cells are strictly above it. Equal values remain together. Require at least 20 cells in each group. All samples and exclusion reasons remain exported, including samples with too many median ties. This is a relative contrast within a tumour, not a claim that half its cells have biologically abnormal CNV burden. A median split preserves power without fitting an outcome-dependent cutpoint.

TCGA burden is the fraction of covered autosomal bases in segments with absolute log2 ratio at least `0.20`. Segment length is `end-start+1` after the explicit shared-endpoint deduplication and the denominator is total covered autosomal length, not an invented complete-genome denominator. Chromosomes X/Y are excluded to reduce sex-related effects. A fixed 0.20 threshold describes a moderate relative alteration and is not calibrated to maximize an MP association. Split tumours at the cohort median FGA: low is at or below, high is strictly above; ties remain together. Cohort sample counts and exact cutpoint are retained. FGA can be affected by purity, ploidy and segmentation. It is not an absolute cell-level copy-number count.

Sensitivity tables repeat continuous TCGA associations for absolute log2 thresholds `0.10` and `0.30`, and for length-weighted root-mean-square log2 signal. These are explicitly named secondary metrics, never silent replacements for the primary FGA definition. No algorithm or input fallback is permitted.

## Effects, tests and uncertainty

Within each scATLAS sample and MP, standardize cell UCell scores by that sample's mean and sample SD, then calculate the high-minus-low mean standardized difference. Each sample contributes one effect irrespective of cell count. Pooled and per-study effects are the equal-sample mean. Cell numbers are not the inferential N. Zero-SD MP/sample scores produce undefined standardized effects, remain represented and are excluded only from that MP's test. At least five finite sample effects and at least one nonzero effect are required for the two-sided one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test against zero (`exact=FALSE`). The displayed mean and Wilcoxon location test summarize related but not identical features of the effect distribution.

TCGA MP scores are the parent's current bulk GSVA scores. Standardize each MP across the whole TCGA cohort, then calculate high-minus-low mean standardized activity. Test the two tumour groups with a two-sided unpaired Wilcoxon test (`exact=FALSE`); require five samples in each group. Different standardization denominators mean scATLAS and TCGA standardized effects are comparable direction summaries, not interchangeable effect parameters.

Bootstrap intervals use 1,000 resamples and percentile 2.5%/97.5% quantiles, seed 42. Resample complete sample effects for scATLAS. Resample tumour samples independently within the fixed high/low TCGA groups; grouping cutpoints remain fixed to describe the observed contrast. scATLAS intervals require five finite effects; smaller studies still appear with available descriptive points and missing inference. BH adjustment spans all 17 MPs separately for pooled scATLAS, each study and TCGA. Asterisks in burden forest/rectangle displays denote BH FDR below 0.05.

Continuous sensitivity avoids the median-split loss of information. Compute per-sample Spearman rho between individual-cell burden and MP UCell, requiring five finite pairs and variable inputs. Summarize scATLAS rho with an equal-sample mean and test sample rhos against zero using the same signed-rank approach. TCGA uses Spearman correlation of continuous FGA and bulk MP GSVA. Correct all 17 MPs within each dataset/scope. Continuous scATLAS rho is an average of within-sample correlations; TCGA rho is one between-sample correlation.

## Limitations

The analysis is observational, with no causal attribution. RNA-derived CN signal and RNA-derived MP scores can share expression noise or biology; scATLAS inferred CNV is not direct single-cell DNA measurement. Tumour purity, stromal mixing, ploidy and gene coverage differ across platforms. Median grouping is relative and discards information, so continuous associations accompany it. Each `orig.ident` is an independent sample unit; known repeat patient specimens are not automatically collapsed, and results must not be described as a verified number of independent patients. Study-specific views and equal sample weights reduce study/cell-count dominance but do not remove confounding from quality, clinical factors or study. Bulk TCGA and within-sample single-cell contrasts should be interpreted together with these different estimands.

Standalone cached rendering must load ggplot2 before reading/drawing saved gtables so gtable drawing methods are registered. The shared TCGA helper now initializes that namespace explicitly; otherwise a caller without plotting packages attached can produce blank pages despite successful source/statistic checks. Page text checks caught this issue, and the corrected independent validator regenerates every PDF from the unchanged analysis cache.

## Current three-page presentation and outer-tertile sensitivity

This revision supersedes the historical five-PDF layouts recorded above. Page 1 retains continuous Spearman associations. The pooled scATLAS column and TCGA EAC column each occupy their own labelled, bordered facet, separated from the individual-study block by 1.3-line gutters. Pages 2 and 3 follow the clinical boxplot layout: every MP on the x-axis, dodged low/high boxes, sample-level dots, sample-count legends and BH-FDR stars (* below 0.05, ** below 0.01, *** below 0.001). The box centre is the median and hinges are the first/third quartiles of plotted values; each scATLAS plotted value itself is an arithmetic mean UCell score within that sample/burden group.

Primary grouping remains the median burden, not the mean: low <= median, high > median, within each scATLAS sample and across the TCGA cohort, respectively. The new clinical-style scATLAS page tests the plotted raw paired sample means using Wilcoxon signed-rank on raw high-minus-low differences. TCGA uses unpaired Wilcoxon rank-sum on raw GSVA scores. Neither uses a t test. BH correction spans all 17 MPs separately in each cohort. These boxplot statistics are exported in `tables/Auto_burden_boxplot_tests.csv` and the results RDS. They are separate from the retained forest's signed-rank tests on within-sample standardized differences; previous separate boxplots annotated those standardized tests. This distinction is explicit to prevent interpreting a raw-mean test as a standardized-effect test.

Continuous Spearman remains primary because it retains all observed burden ranks and tests a monotonic association without an arbitrary dichotomization. It does not assume a linear relationship in the original burden scale; it can miss non-monotonic patterns. Dichotomization loses information (Altman and Royston, BMJ 2006, https://www.bmj.com/content/332/7549/1080.1.full). An outer-tertile contrast asks a different, extreme-group question and removes the middle third, so it is supplied as a predeclared sensitivity analysis rather than selected according to which P value is smaller.

For scATLAS sensitivity, compute type-7 burden quantiles 1/3 and 2/3 within every sample. Low <= q1, high > q2; the middle is excluded. Equal values remain together. Require at least 20 cells in each retained extreme group, recording every sample's counts and eligibility. Calculate raw MP means for each retained sample/group, then paired signed-rank tests across samples, pooled and per study, with five finite sample pairs required. For TCGA compute cohort FGA tertiles and use unpaired rank-sum tests between low and high tumours. Quantiles, counts, exclusions and eligibility are in `tables/Auto_burden_tertile_groups.csv`; scATLAS means in `tables/Auto_burden_tertile_sample_means.csv`; full effects, P values and 17-MP within-scope BH FDR in `tables/Auto_burden_tertile_tests.csv`. These sources also persist in the results RDS. Reduced scATLAS sample eligibility under the extreme-group rule can itself change precision, so a sensitivity P value is not evidence that this grouping is intrinsically superior. The page 2/3 boxplots remain median-based; no unannounced substitution with tertile results occurs.

Independent validation reconstructs raw-value paired/unpaired boxplot tests, tertile cutpoints/counts/group means, all BH families, display-source sizes and the three heatmap sections, in addition to the original cell/source/FGA checks. It verifies one retained forest page and three combined presentation pages, and asserts the three explicitly removed PDFs are absent after cached replot. Cached replot cannot recreate those deleted exports.
