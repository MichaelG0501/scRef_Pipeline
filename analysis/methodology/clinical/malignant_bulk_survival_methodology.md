# Malignant-compartment deconvolution and cross-cohort survival analysis

## Scope and interpretation

The maintained entry points are `analysis/clinical/Auto_bayesprism_bulk_deconvolution.R` and `analysis/clinical/Auto_malignant_bulk_survival.R`. They test whether current metaprogramme (MP) activity, state-signature activity, and inferred state composition show concordant overall-survival associations in OCCAMS and TCGA oesophageal adenocarcinoma. The patient is the statistical unit. Single cells construct the reference and are not independent survival observations.

This is a retrospective follow-up to earlier analyses of both cohorts. Biological expectations are hypotheses, not selection criteria: proliferation and stress-associated signatures are expected to associate with higher hazard, whereas lineage-associated signatures are expected to associate with lower hazard. Every prespecified feature remains visible, including opposite directions and null results. Neither cohort is an untouched external validation set. Concordant statistical findings provide retrospective cross-cohort support and still require independent validation.

The primary comparison retains the exact previously selected OCCAMS surgery-pathway and TCGA surgery-Yes patient lists. This aligns the operational subgroup definitions; it does not establish identical treatment exposure, staging, tissue timing, or survival origins. There is no propensity-score matching, patient pairing between cohorts, outcome-driven cutpoint optimization, or search for a model that produces an expected direction.

## Cohort construction and overall survival

Deconvolution and parent-population expression scoring retain exactly 281 OCCAMS subjects with `Annotation_Compatible_Below_50_Flag == no`, and all 88 TCGA EAC primary-tumour samples in the corrected workbook. Each cohort contributes one RNA sample per patient. TCGA identity is joined through explicit workbook and GDC identifiers. Single-cell sample identity always uses `orig.ident`.

The primary surgery comparison preserves 68 OCCAMS patients classified as curative intent, surgery modality, and RNA metadata matched, and 72 TCGA patients classified as `Flowchart_Surgery_Status_Final == Yes` within `Surgery group`. The three existing OCCAMS exclusions remain fixed in the survival script. Primary selection does not expand to the four OCCAMS surgery-pathway cases lacking the required RNA metadata match or to TCGA `Yes (after neoadjuvant)` cases. The new membership table is checked against the prior exact identifiers, not merely the same counts.

OCCAMS overall survival uses `Survival_Days` with `Vital_Status == Dead` as the event. TCGA uses explicitly recorded overall-survival months and death status from its clinical snapshot, converting months to days using 30.4375 days/month. Unknown status or missing/nonpositive follow-up is not imputed as an observed death. Endpoint eligibility and covariate completeness are recorded separately, so clinical adjustment cannot silently change the reported denominator. Deaths from all causes define the endpoint; the analysis does not infer cause-specific survival.

The OCCAMS neoadjuvant parent contains 157 RNA-matched patients, with 77 biopsy and 80 surgery specimens. These are distinct patients, not paired longitudinal samples. They are analysed separately as secondary context because treatment and sampling can change expression. Broad all-cohort analyses are also secondary and cannot substitute for the fixed surgery comparison.

Recorded preoperative treatment is missing for most TCGA patients. Absence of a treatment record is not proof of no treatment. The OCCAMS survival-duration field does not provide a verified common time origin in the supplied workbook. Absolute survival curves must therefore not be interpreted as time-origin-harmonized comparisons between cohorts. Post-treatment and surgery-based selection can introduce survivor/selection bias; unavailable collection-to-origin intervals prevent a rigorous common landmark or delayed-entry analysis. The new workflow does not repeat recurrence-free survival because the available cross-cohort recurrence definitions and timing do not support a newly harmonized endpoint.

## Corrected single-cell reference

Malignancy is assigned from `meta_full_epi.rds`: both `malignant_level_1` and `malignant_level_2` are retained as malignant, preserving the upstream pipeline contract. Every canonical state-labelled cell must be among those upstream malignant cells. All 96,519 canonical malignant cells remain malignant irrespective of state availability.

The merged atlas contains another 9,261 epithelial cells outside this malignant metadata. Their exact cell identifiers and `orig.ident` are joined to the existing `ref_outs/by_samples/<orig.ident>/<orig.ident>_epi.rds` metadata. Normal epithelial reference eligibility requires both `classification == cna_non_malignant` and `malignant_clus == non_malignant_clus`, corresponding to the strongest nonmalignant category in `Malignancy.R`. This retains 3,857 normal-reference cells. The remaining 5,404 cells have discordant, uncertain, unavailable or upstream-QC-excluded calls and do not enter the reference. No alternate file or classifier replaces absent calls. Exact source paths, presence and hashes are retained in `epithelial_source_manifest.csv`; cell-level classification, source status and exclusion reasons remain in `reference_cell_audit.csv`. These sources are read directly by the maintained producer, without dependence on a temporary audit.

The reference contains one `Malignant` cell type with eight states: the five biological states, `Hybrid`, `Unresolved`, and `Malignant state unassigned`. The final category retains malignant cells absent from the current state vector without inventing a state. The source audit identified 21,171 such cells: 12,350 `malignant_level_1` and 8,821 `malignant_level_2`. Treating their missing state labels as evidence of nonmalignancy would create an incorrect normal epithelial reference. The older OCCAMS-only deconvolution incorrectly put these state-unassigned malignant cells into its nonmalignant epithelial reference; its fitted fractions and inferred expression are not reused in this workflow.

Other compartments use the atlas `celltype_update` labels, replacing punctuation for stable BayesPrism labels. Cells marked `unresolved_inconsistent` are excluded. Sparse raw counts are summed by reference state before BayesPrism `input.type='GEP'`; normalization therefore reflects total counts within each labelled group. Each state requires at least 20 cells. Genes must be detected in at least 20 retained reference cells. The cell-level reference audit retains `orig.ident`, original type, malignancy, assigned type/state and inclusion status.

The reference is pooled across donors; it is not donor-balanced and is not a leave-one-donor-out validation. High-RNA cells and larger contributing samples influence the summed profiles. Per-state donor coverage and malignant-reference profile correlations are retained for review. Normal epithelial eligibility is supported by computational CNA and cluster calls, not independent histological verification. Similar normal and malignant profiles can still compete for reads. Upstream `malignant_level_2` calls also retain their original uncertainty. Correcting the known state-coverage error does not remove these limitations or prove that inferred expression is purified malignant RNA.

## Gene harmonization and BayesPrism estimation

OCCAMS and reference Ensembl identifiers map through pinned GENCODE v19. TCGA uses the gene symbols supplied with its pinned GENCODE-v36 STAR files. Duplicate symbols are summed, unmapped features are excluded, and all raw bulk counts must be finite, nonnegative integers with positive library totals. Gene identifiers, sample identifiers and source checksums are checked explicitly.

The prepared common gene universe is the intersection of reference, OCCAMS and TCGA symbols. Mitochondrial genes matching `^MT-`, ribosomal genes matching `^RPL[0-9]` or `^RPS[0-9]`, and GENCODE-v19 X/Y genes are excluded. Genes must have positive total counts in the reference and each bulk cohort. This deliberately removes expression classes susceptible to technical or sex-composition effects. At least 1,000 genes must remain. Annotation differences between GENCODE releases remain a limitation of symbol-based harmonization.

BayesPrism estimates separate cohort fits using the same reference and preprocessing, `key='Malignant'`, fixed seed 22062026, and the installed BayesPrism version recorded in the cache contract. The standard highly abundant-gene rule is a within-sample read fraction above 0.01 in more than 10% of samples. The union of genes flagged in either cohort is removed from both. `new.prism(outlier.cut=1, outlier.fraction=1)` then disables a second, cohort-dependent filter; the script asserts identical prepared and inferred gene ordering. The effective inference genes and downstream common scoring genes are auditable. Gibbs sampling receives the explicit seed through `gibbs.control`; the installed defaults use a chain length of 1,000, burn-in of 500, thinning of two and alpha of one. Sampler settings and fitted objects remain saved, rather than retaining only plotted fractions.

The fitted initial posterior provides state fractions and malignant-type expression through `get.fraction(..., which.theta='first', state.or.type='state')` and `get.exp(..., state.or.type='type', cell.name='Malignant')`. In BayesPrism 2.2.3, `get.exp` returns the initial type-specific posterior expression. The updated posterior supplies total malignant-type fractions. Updated cell-state fractions are not available and are not fabricated by multiplying initial states by updated purity.

For each sample, within-malignant state fraction is the initial fraction of that state divided by the sum of all eight initial malignant-state fractions. The denominator retains `Hybrid`, `Unresolved`, and `Malignant state unassigned`. It never closes only the five biological states to 100%. Whole-bulk fractions and initial/updated total malignant fractions are also retained. These quantities represent RNA/read contributions over the retained genes; unequal RNA content prevents interpreting them as directly validated cell counts. Posterior coefficient of variation describes uncertainty conditional on the reference/model, not donor-reference uncertainty or biological accuracy. The resolution table records the median within-malignant fraction, counts below 0.1%, the median initial-state posterior CV and counts with CV above 0.5. These descriptive thresholds do not select patients or hypotheses. Many fractions are near zero with high relative uncertainty; survival coefficients for those features must not be presented as validated associations with accurately measured cell abundance. This interpretation follows the original [BayesPrism study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-022-00356-3) and its [maintained documentation](https://github.com/Danko-Lab/BayesPrism).

Recent benchmarks support checking reference coverage and purity rather than assuming a deconvolution method guarantees biological accuracy. A [2023 breast-cancer benchmark](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41385-5) found BayesPrism among the stronger methods across tumour-purity conditions. The [2026 Statescope study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74997-8) found comparable supervised state recovery for BayesPrism and Statescope in its NSCLC simulations, while also identifying states missed when their profiles were absent from the reference. Those results motivate the explicit unassigned compartment, reference audit and cautious interpretation here; they do not validate this OAC reference or justify changing algorithms after viewing survival significance.

## Signature definitions and paired bulk comparison

The survival feature panel contains 32 features: 17 MPs, five state MP-gene unions, five state top-20 marker signatures, and five biological-state within-malignant fractions. MP2x, MP11c and MP18a are excluded. Cell-cycle MPs MP1, MP5 and MP13+ remain individual MP tests; they are not silently added to the `Classic proliferation` state union. The latter follows the current grouping and contains MP2+.

State MP unions deduplicate genes from their assigned current MPs. Marker signatures retain the first 20 rows per state in the canonical ranked marker file, preserving source order. Genes and states are not chosen using bulk survival results. Every expression signature requires at least five matched genes. Source membership, matched membership and coverage are saved.

Whole-bulk and inferred malignant-expression matrices use a common gene universe across both cohorts and both expression sources. Each sample is transformed to `log2(CPM + 1)` over that common universe. A gene constant in any complete parent compartment is then removed from all four scoring matrices, independently of survival outcomes. The remaining common genes are scored with Gaussian-kernel GSVA using the installed 1.46.0 API. All 281 OCCAMS or 88 TCGA samples define their respective scoring parent populations. Surgery and neoadjuvant subgroup scores are subsets of those fixed parent scores, not recalculated after viewing subgroup outcomes. The same 27 gene sets and score transformation are used for the bulk and inferred malignant sources. This paired processing makes the deconvolution comparison interpretable; it also differs deliberately from historical analyses that used OCCAMS TMM-logCPM and TCGA raw TPM with different scoring populations.

## Statistical models, multiplicity and diagnostics

The primary features remain continuous. Cox proportional-hazards models report hazard ratios, 95% confidence intervals, two-sided P values, eligible patients, deaths, model status and proportional-hazards diagnostics. Expression-score effects are per one subgroup standard deviation; fractions are per 10 percentage points. Score scales are determined once among endpoint-eligible subgroup patients and retained across adjustment models. A hazard ratio above one means higher hazard with greater activity or fraction. It does not establish treatment response or a causal effect.

The primary model adjusts for categorical pathological stage. Unadjusted models on all endpoint-eligible patients and on the same stage-complete patients provide distinct sensitivity comparisons. Stage is not assumed to have a linear ordinal effect. Unknown stage is missing, not assigned a numeric category or imputed. Age, sex and categorical stage form a separate sensitivity adjustment when estimable. A stage-plus-inferred-malignant-fraction sensitivity assesses dependence on the estimated malignant RNA contribution. This fraction is model-derived and cannot be treated as measured histological purity. These adjustments cannot eliminate residual treatment confounding or reconcile different staging editions in the underlying cohorts.

Each fitted model requires at least 20 eligible patients, at least 10 deaths, at least five deaths per fitted coefficient, a full-rank design, and at least two deaths in every covariate factor category. A non-estimable model is retained with its reason; no simpler model substitutes for its labelled specification. Numerical failures stop execution. The thresholds are transparent operational safeguards, not proof of unbiased small-sample estimation.

Benjamini–Hochberg correction covers the fixed 32-feature family within a cohort, expression source and model specification. Cross-cohort evidence uses a conservative conjunction: for same-sign fitted effects, the larger of the two two-sided P values is the conjunction P; discordant effects receive one. Benjamini–Hochberg correction then covers all 32 conjunction tests. Both-cohort nominal P<0.05 is recorded separately and is not equivalent to FDR-supported replication. A pooled estimate significant because of one large cohort does not establish replication in the other.

Schoenfeld-residual proportional-hazards tests are recorded. Natural-spline nonlinearity checks compare three degrees of freedom against the linear feature model when at least 30 events and ten events per model coefficient permit estimation. Before fitting, the spline design must be full rank and have maximum diagonal leverage below 0.5, calculated from its QR decomposition including the intercept. This outcome-independent design-support safeguard prevents a spline direction from being determined mostly by one patient; it is an operational eligibility threshold, not an association-selection rule. Ineligible diagnostics retain their reason and leverage in the results. The primary linear specification is unchanged, and unexpected warnings from an eligible fit still stop execution. For each biological-state fraction, a separate compositional sensitivity uses the log2 ratio of that state to the geometric mean of the other seven malignant states. No pseudocount is added. Fraction effects are relative to the remaining malignant composition; fitting all fractions together as independent unconstrained predictors would be inappropriate.

The separate clinical-stage context fits categorical pathological stage alone in each primary cohort, reporting stages II–IV relative to stage I, confidence intervals, the overall likelihood-ratio P value and global proportional-hazards test. These clinical reference contrasts are descriptive and are not part of the 32 molecular-feature replication family. Stage Kaplan–Meier panels show the corresponding stage-complete patient and death counts.

Fixed median splits are used only to illustrate state Kaplan–Meier curves with risk tables. The split is not optimized against survival and is not the principal inferential test. Neither medians nor quartiles are searched to obtain a smaller P value. The continuous modelling, full model disclosure, missing-data audit and transparent reporting follow [REMARK](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1001216). Because multiple sensitivities are shown, a favourable secondary model cannot replace the designated primary inference.

## Execution, caching and verification

All substantial reads, deconvolution, modelling and figure rendering run on PBS compute nodes in `dmtcp`. The submission-only launchers are `job_scripts/clinical/Auto_bayesprism_bulk_deconvolution.sh` and `job_scripts/clinical/Auto_malignant_bulk_survival.sh`; they are local execution aids, not analytical dependencies. Scheduler stdout/stderr remain in the project root. The deconvolution order is `prepare` (4 CPUs, 96 GB, one hour), then independent `OCCAMS` and `TCGA` fits (16 CPUs, 96 GB, six hours each), then `qc` (one CPU, 8 GB, 30 minutes); survival follows successful extraction for both cohorts.

The reference/cache contracts contain source hashes, analysis settings and package versions. The survival cache also stores the statistical function bodies, so a model change invalidates it while a purely graphical change permits replotting. `SCREF_FORCE_REBUILD=TRUE` explicitly rebuilds the selected task when inputs/settings change. `SCREF_REPLOT_ONLY=TRUE` uses persistent extracted data for deconvolution QC and the persistent analysis cache for survival figures; it does not silently rerun the statistical analysis. Force and replot modes are mutually exclusive. Missing or incompatible required inputs cause an immediate error, with no alternative input or algorithm.

Before accepting outputs, check patient/sample uniqueness and exact cohort membership, reference cell accounting, matched inference/scoring genes, finite nonnegative extracted values, and closure of whole-bulk and within-malignant fractions to one within tolerance 1e-6. Initial malignant-type fraction must equal the sum of all eight malignant states. Confirm all 17 MPs and all five biological states remain represented. Check endpoint/covariate exclusions, event counts, complete feature/model rows and multiplicity calculations independently from the source tables. PDF review checks page count, clipping, readable text and matching data labels. A zero PBS exit status alone does not establish completion. Compact current summaries are also written below `updates/new_updates/summaries/` and remain below 100 KB.

## Four-method display requested on 29 October 2026

The two final PDFs show one overview page each, with four-page detailed forests in supporting 07/08: continuous, median split, Q1 versus Q4, and optimal cutpoint. Each page retains all 17 MPs or all 15 state features. Colours follow the canonical state palette, including purple for stress-adaptive programmes and grey for cell cycle. Each overview estimate reports HR, P, BH q and an explicit significance category; the corresponding supporting forest adds its 95% confidence interval. HR above one denotes higher hazard with greater activity/fraction, or in the high group.

These unadjusted displays supplement the primary stage-adjusted continuous analysis, retained in supporting figures 05 and 06. Median high means strictly above the median. Quartile comparisons retain values at or below Q1 and at or above Q3. Optimal-cut high means at or above the candidate threshold. The 13 candidate quantiles are 0.20 to 0.80 in steps of 0.05, matching the original workflow. Binary eligibility follows the original safeguards: at least 20 patients, five deaths, five patients per group and one death per group. Missing support is explicitly unestimated. Unexpected numerical warnings stop execution.

The optimal threshold minimizes two-sided Cox Wald P among eligible candidates. Its inferential P is conservatively corrected by multiplying the selected raw P by all 13 planned thresholds, capped at one. BH correction across the fixed 32 features follows this search correction, separately within each cohort and method. Raw candidate tests and selected thresholds remain fully tabulated. The selected HR/CI is descriptive and remains subject to selection bias; the uncorrected minimum P is not presented as valid significance. Comparisons across four methods are exploratory and do not replace primary inference. A separate fingerprinted four-method cache permits plot-only reruns without altering the original continuous model cache.
